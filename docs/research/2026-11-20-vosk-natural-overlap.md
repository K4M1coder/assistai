# Natural French competing-speech probe

## Hypothesis and protocol

The running Vosk recognizer may lose a target utterance when another person speaks at the same time. This matters even with push-to-talk: the button controls when recording occurs, not whose voice is recorded. The experiment directly sends fixed, non-personal research audio to the already-running loopback Vosk WebSocket at approximately real time; it does not open a microphone or exercise the browser's noise gate, push-to-talk, echo cancellation or physical room acoustics.

Two French test utterances originate from [Google FLEURS](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/fleurs), attributed to A. Conneau et al. (2022), CC BY 4.0, using the [pinned re-encoded fixture dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/podscripter-project/test-fixtures/blob/6e1c1eced6e68bb35b1f1d89c56478229201a2f7/README.md). The target is utterance `17559822372056259463`, reference `Cela semble raisonnable, car la Terre n'a pas l'impression de bouger, n'est-ce pas ?`; the competing voice is the first 5.04 seconds of utterance `118005971910520590`. Both are 16 kHz mono PCM16 and their exact source hashes and metadata are documented in [the earlier natural-speech record](2026-11-17-kyutai-persistent.md). Respect attribution and license requirements if redistributing audio.

`$env:PYTHONIOENCODING='utf-8'; .\.venv-voice\Scripts\python.exe scripts\probe-vosk-natural-overlap.py` multiplies the target PCM by 8 and adds the beginning of the other recording at gain 0, 32 or 64, respectively. These gains compensate for the files' different levels; the script rejects clipping. The signals are summed without room impulse responses or spatial processing, and the competitor is cropped to the target's duration. The first five seconds of the longer second utterance need not correspond to its full transcript. The service reported `Vosk`, `vosk-model-small-fr-0.22`, French, 16 kHz. No model was added or loaded for this experiment.

## Results, 2026-11-20

The target RMS was 1,417.45 PCM units across all cases. Normalized target word error rate (WER) uses the tokenizer in `scripts/probe-asr-coding.py`; lower is better.

| Competing gain | Competing RMS | Peak | Target WER | Transcript |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 0 | 22,632 | 0.059 | `cela semble raisonnable que la terre n'a pas l'impression de bouger n'est-ce pas` |
| 32 | 820.36 | 22,600 | 0.882 | `cela semble régner rap interne impressionne allégé paresseux des bottes d'équipe` |
| 64 | 1,640.72 | 22,568 | 0.824 | `cela semble premièrement à la plupart des cavaliers porte des bottes d'équipe` |

The hashes of the actual mixed PCM are, respectively, `8ccebef039fa97a81a21889e7763da592fba8c91ec040e1880d25a73f7628b75`, `14d8c481ff3d71eefce64a7aae961d27bfb7adaf3b8b8958f2e4bad3e2f85840` and `17ea273dbface81754a0519a03816eba4337f2289aab899d19e6a1d9dfa290e8`. The two voices overlap from the start; no actual loudspeaker playback was used. The competing speech severely degraded this target in these two mixtures and influenced the second transcription. The gain-64 WER being slightly lower than gain-32 does not imply better target recognition; neither was accurate. This one pair of utterances is not a population benchmark, a TV/music test, or proof of speaker isolation failure for every room. Push-to-talk reduces unwanted capture periods but cannot isolate overlapping speakers. Test candidate suppression against the same mixtures and negative controls before integrating it.

## Exploratory target-speaker extraction on disjoint segments

Hypothesis: the previously tested CPU VoxLock extractor can preserve the intended natural speaker while attenuating a competing voice, and selecting the competing enrollment should reverse that behavior. This diagnostic uses the same two source recordings but a **different, shorter mixture**: the first three seconds of each source provide its own enrollment, and samples after three seconds form the 2.04-second mixture. The enrollment and mixture samples do not overlap, but are from the same recordings, so this is not a test of cross-session enrollment or generalization. The target and competitor gains are 8 and 64, without clipping. It does not reproduce the full-length WER cases above.

The first run of `powershell -NoProfile -File scripts/run-voxlock-natural-overlap.ps1` failed before extraction because the initial two-second enrollment was shorter than VoxLock's three-second minimum (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-194926-531.*`). After changing the split to three seconds, a second guarded run completed. Its floating-point mixture SHA-256 was `db4edd943b2fa80a9f848ae3046e7c1ef24256ac14dbd10a07f7559ce2e2e904`. Input correlations with target and competitor were 0.4600 and 0.8973. With target enrollment, output correlations were 0.9587 and -0.1098, and Vosk returned `l'impression de bouger n'est-ce pas`. With competitor enrollment, correlations were 0.1515 and 0.9755, and Vosk returned `après cavaliers porte des bottes d'équipe`. The mixture itself transcribed as `après cavaliers porte des bottes d'équipe`; the clean target segment transcribed as `prison de bouger n'est-ce pas`. Correlation is the signed maximum absolute normalized cross-correlation within ±200 ms; it is not a measure of intelligibility or a calibrated separation score. Short ASR transcriptions are observations, not an independently checked reference.

Processing the 2.04 seconds took 1.570 seconds with target enrollment and 2.196 seconds with competitor enrollment, excluding model loading, enrollment and subsequent recognition; neither is a chunk-latency or sustained real-time guarantee. The 13 whole-system samples (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-194944-191.*`) peaked at 11,476,779,008 dedicated GPU bytes and 167,997,440 shared, with minimum available RAM 10,920,112,128. The extractor ran on CPU with its INT8 ONNX models in system RAM and was not combined with native speech or a live agent. This promising single same-recording example is insufficient to enable the feature, prove voice identity, satisfy the all-models-in-VRAM goal, or establish performance with room acoustics, television, or real speakers. Longer, independent enrollments and utterances, more speakers and negative controls are needed.

## Separate-recording enrollment

The [MLS-Sidon dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sarulab-speech/mls_sidon) describes restored [Multilingual LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/94/) audio and speaker identifiers, with CC BY 4.0 attribution requirements. `scripts/setup-mls-natural-overlap.py` reads only the first selected entries of the pinned French test shard at revision `a9abb3a85d5ca67f92ed29f65003b420169e2732`, caps compressed input at 12 MiB, verifies metadata and does not extract archive paths. It saved two excerpts from speaker `1406`, chapter `1028`, at separate non-overlapping times in the same LibriVox audiobook chapter. Their FLAC SHA-256 values are `8346625854eb7c3611febabf50747e1e315597c357390197740424f04c71c260` and `e769454a1922457df8580baf95ca2bfd4b0ffeb526f1ac61fa43c5ab77170431`. Audio and metadata remain in ignored cache. Cite W. Nakata et al. (2025), *Sidon*, and V. Pratap et al. (2020), *MLS*, with source and license if redistributing. The recordings are separate time segments from the same audiobook session, not independent recording environments.

On 2026-11-20, `powershell -NoProfile -File scripts/run-voxlock-natural-overlap.ps1 -CrossRecording` enrolled the first six seconds of the first MLS excerpt and mixed seven seconds from the second with seven disjoint seconds of the FLEURS female recording. A separate three-second part of that FLEURS recording provided the competing enrollment. MLS was resampled from 48 kHz to 16 kHz; source RMS levels were matched across the mixture, and common scaling was 1.0. Mixture float32 SHA-256 was `736b4436d616f430fab51d7dd2d9eaddea4eacf62e87c50f572ccb3e10583aad`. Input correlations with target and competitor were both 0.7094. With target enrollment, correlations were 0.9848 and 0.0433; Vosk returned `dans la mort certaine idée de génie choisi seulement de quelle sorte que tu veux que je te fasse mourir`. With competing enrollment, correlations were 0.0553 and 0.9794; Vosk returned `des cavaliers porte des bottes d'équitation avec chantal une semelle lisse est assez étroite`. The unprocessed mixture combined words from both speakers; clean-target Vosk returned `non ta mort certaine dit de génie choisi seulement de quelle sorte tu veux que je te fasse mourir`. These are automated transcripts, not human listening or a verified reference for the seven-second crop.

The seven seconds took 6.856 seconds for target enrollment and 7.424 for the competing control, excluding model load, enrollment and ASR. This is close to or slower than real time even before integration overhead, and it does not measure worst-case chunk latency. The 27 whole-system samples (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-195231-088.*`) peaked at 11,431,137,280 dedicated GPU bytes and 202,272,768 shared, with minimum available RAM 10,974,064,640. CPU-resident INT8 models, synthetic digital mixing, one target speaker, and one competing speaker limit the result. This does not validate real-room interruption, independent-session enrollment, many-speaker performance, concurrent model workload or the all-models-in-VRAM requirement.

## Offline chunk timing comparison

The [VoxLock author](https://pypi.org/project/voxlock/1.3.1/) reports real-time CPU performance on a different host; this does not predict performance on this machine. On 2026-11-20, three sequential guarded runs used the same seven-second mixture and target enrollment, 20 ms input blocks, INT8 CPU models, and 1, 2 or 4 ONNX Runtime threads: `powershell -NoProfile -File scripts/run-voxlock-natural-overlap.ps1 -CrossRecording -Threads <1|2|4> -TargetOnly`. Each processed 350 blocks offline, followed by a flush; the timings exclude model loading, enrollment and ASR. This is one uncontrolled run per setting, not a statistically robust benchmark.

| Threads | Total including flush | Median push | 95th percentile push | Maximum push |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 8.473 s | 25.15 ms | 30.17 ms | 31.85 ms |
| 2 | 6.809 s | 18.91 ms | 24.02 ms | 25.41 ms |
| 4 | 5.431 s | 15.68 ms | 19.31 ms | 21.39 ms |

The target correlation was 0.9848 in each run and competitor correlation 0.0433, 0.0433 and 0.0432; Vosk transcripts were similar but not identical. The four-thread sample had a 95th percentile below the 20 ms input interval but still had a slower maximum block. These measurements do not include input scheduling, concurrent native and agent inference, enrollment, transport or output buffering and do not guarantee sustained real-time operation. No physical devices were used. Whole-system samples for the one/two/four-thread trials (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-195456-864.*`, `-195526-000.*`, `-195558-392.*`) peaked respectively at 11,433,431,040 / 11,447,185,408 / 11,434,516,480 dedicated GPU bytes and 200,896,512 / 200,896,512 / 136,343,552 shared; minimum available RAM was 11,126,108,160 / 11,112,366,080 / 11,115,581,440 bytes. The CPU models reside in RAM and these snapshots do not prove no spill or isolation quality in real rooms.

Two additional sequential runs used the identical mixture and enrollment with `-ChunkMs 40 -TargetOnly`, first `-Threads 1`, then `-Threads 2`. One thread processed seven seconds in 6.643 seconds with median/p95/maximum push times 36.49 / 43.34 / 45.22 ms; two threads took 4.980 seconds with 31.87 / 33.32 / 33.92 ms. Target correlations were 0.9847 in both and competing correlations 0.0433 / 0.0434; ASR outputs remained similar but not identical. This suggests a throughput tradeoff worth testing, but collecting a 40 ms block adds buffering compared with 20 ms, and the offline unpaced timing excludes enrollment, full-system concurrency and audio I/O. One run per setting does not establish sustained real-time behavior. The 17 / 15 whole-system samples (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-195845-865.*`, `-195911-031.*`) peaked at 11,444,756,480 / 11,451,121,664 dedicated GPU bytes and 134,967,296 shared; minimum available RAM was 11,134,029,824 / 11,140,276,224 bytes. No native voice model was loaded for these runs.

On 2026-11-20, `powershell -NoProfile -File scripts/run-voxlock-natural-overlap.ps1 -CrossRecording -Threads 2 -ChunkMs 40 -TargetOnly -Paced` replayed the same seven-second mixture on a monotonic 40 ms input schedule. The 175 push calls took 32.67 ms median, 33.60 ms p95 and 34.61 ms maximum; measured from each block's scheduled availability until its push returned, delays were 32.88 / 33.96 / 35.23 ms respectively. Total paced replay including final flush was 7.053 seconds. This metric starts **after** a full block is available; it excludes the up-to-40-ms input accumulation, model algorithmic delay, enrollment, audio capture, transport, recognition and speech output. The fixture was replayed, not captured from a microphone, and the native model and agent were not concurrently active. Source correlations were 0.9847 for target and 0.0434 for competing speech, and Vosk returned `dans la mort certaine idée de génie choisi seulement de quelle sorte que tu veux que je te fasse mourir`. One short run cannot establish sustained latency or real-room isolation. Seventeen system samples (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-200040-336.*`) peaked at 11,451,121,664 dedicated GPU bytes and 134,967,296 shared, with minimum available RAM 11,133,964,288; the extractor remained CPU-resident.

These timings exclude enrollment, block accumulation, input/output devices, transport, recognition and native voice inference. The single small task is not representative of sustained coding workloads. Eighteen whole-system samples (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-natural-20261120-200224-476.*`) peaked at 11,453,489,152 dedicated GPU bytes and 134,967,296 shared, with minimum available RAM 10,240,831,488. ## DirectML placement hypothesis

The installed VoxLock 1.3.1 Python wrapper constructs ONNX Runtime sessions without selecting an execution provider; the experiments above used CPU. Its [author's published GPU figure](https://pypi.org/project/voxlock/1.3.1/) refers to FP32 on NVIDIA L4 with CUDA, not Windows DirectML on Intel Arc. To test a possible Arc path without silently accepting CPU graph nodes, `powershell -NoProfile -File scripts/run-voxlock-directml-probe.ps1` initializes the cached enrollment and INT8 chunk ONNX graphs with DirectML device 0 and `session.disable_cpu_ep_fallback=1`. It verifies graph hashes, uses the isolated ONNX Runtime DirectML 1.23.0 environment described in the [Piper record](2026-11-19-piper-directml-candidate.md), and performs no audio inference. A successful session initialization alone would not establish physical residency, speed, quality or complete real-time integration.

On 2026-11-20 three attempts were refused by the unmodified preflight before a process was started; shared GPU usage was 137,719,808 bytes, above the 134,217,728-byte limit. No result for graph compatibility exists yet. The graph hashes are `81f305c9b7a32f13d1401f53bb4c6301826a607c9e807e60912f36ef1b9fae29` for `enroll.onnx` and `c50ffdc8d723fd4f91cb08889f3d0143491f95967b705f35c444a0b4afe88172` for `chunk_int8.onnx`; they are local cache artifacts, not committed. When headroom allows, inspect both graph results and resource samples, then test audio and latency only if initialization succeeds. Do not infer that a provider-listed session has all weights resident in dedicated VRAM.

A subsequent guarded attempt on 2026-11-20 passed preflight at 133,591,040 shared GPU bytes and 10,777,075,712 available RAM. ONNX Runtime 1.23.0 initialized `enroll.onnx` with CPU fallback disabled in 2.062 seconds; `session.get_providers()` still listed both DirectML and the automatically registered CPU provider, which is not itself a residency proof. Initializing `chunk_int8.onnx` then failed: ONNX Runtime reported nodes assigned to the default CPU provider while fallback was disabled. The process exited 1 and performed no extraction. Three system samples (`.cache/assistant/voxlock-directml-20261120-200335-300.*`) peaked at 11,444,973,568 dedicated GPU bytes and 134,090,752 shared, with minimum available RAM 10,547,613,696. Thus this exact INT8 graph/runtime combination cannot be used as an entirely DirectML graph under this check. It remains unknown whether alternative precision, graph conversion, mixed providers or another backend would meet latency and residency goals. Sampled counters cannot prove placement or exclude transients.
